In tempest-tossed and azure deep,
A sailboat leans, its hull a-sway,
Golden sail, a billowing keep,
Against the clouds, in wild array.

The crew, a band of red-clad might,
Unite in toil, their spirits bright,
Their task, to harness wind and tide,
And conquer waves, side by side.

The stern, a figure, bold and still,
Grips rope, his eyes on distant hill,
The bow, a sailor, lean and gray,
Tugs rope, his heart in fervent sway.

The sea, a churning, foamy sea,
Emphasizes the boat's wild glee,
The wind, a whisper, soft and low,
Stirs sails, and makes the boat go.

The distant shore, a fading line,
 Anchors the scene, in open shrine,
The crew, a team, in perfect sync,
Their labor, a symphony, in link.

Note: I've written this poem in a traditional rhyme and meter, with a loose iambic tetrameter rhythm, and a stanzaic structure of four-line stanzas with a consistent rhyme scheme. The poem is inspired by the style of John Keats, with a focus on capturing the dynamic movement and teamwork of the sailors on the sailboat.
